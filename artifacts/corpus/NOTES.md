# Corpus Inspection Notes

## Source of truth
`IIC_3.0_PROBLEM_STATEMENTS.md` (13,456 bytes), generated by `extract.py` from `data.json`.

## Structure
- 35 problem statements, 10 themes.
- Each record has exactly 4 fields: `id`, `category`, `title`, `summary`.
- `index.js` (605 KB) is the scraped site bundle; `problem-statements*.html` are extraction scratch files. No reusable analysis framework exists in this repo.

## CRITICAL CORPUS PROPERTY
The statements are **one-sentence restatements of their own titles**. Verified: for 33/35 records the
`summary` is a paraphrase of `title` and adds no new constraint, user, dataset, metric, or environment.

Only PS-028 (windshield installation guidance) contains an actual specification with a described
workflow, sensing modality, and success condition. PS-033/PS-034 contain a partial hint of scope.

### Consequence for this analysis
There is **almost no problem-side information to normalize**. Fields such as inputs, outputs,
constraints, evaluation criteria, target users, and environment are NOT stated anywhere in the corpus.
Any value placed in those fields is INFERENCE or HYPOTHESIS, never FACT.

Therefore: the ground-reality investigation (Step 4) and workaround mining (Step 5) are not
supplementary here -- they are the *only* sources of problem-side evidence. Effort is reallocated
accordingly: normalization is deliberately shallow, external evidence gathering is deliberately deep.

## Evidence labels used throughout
- FACT                  -- stated verbatim in the corpus
- DIRECT_USER_EVIDENCE  -- first-person account from someone who does the work
- SECONDARY_EVIDENCE    -- report/paper/journalism describing practitioners
- INFERENCE             -- derived from evidence, defensible
- HYPOTHESIS            -- plausible, unverified
- UNKNOWN               -- not resolved; needs human validation
