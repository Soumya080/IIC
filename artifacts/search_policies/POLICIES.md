# Step 10 — Adaptive Search Controller

Seven strategies, derived from the opportunity map rather than from a generic creativity template.
Budget is deliberately uneven: the procurement cluster and the EPR-attestation region receive three
of seven explorers because their evidence is strongest and their gaps are structural.

Every strategy inherits the global blacklist and these two global anti-convergence rules:
- **No candidate whose core contribution is a classifier.** Perception may be a component only.
- **No candidate that adds a ledger under an unverified input.**

---

### E1 — Assumption inversion: move the decision upstream (PS-015/PS-004)
- **Target region:** G1 — the farmer's go/no-go decision at loading time.
- **Avoid:** the procurement centre itself; slot-status displays; CV grading.
- **Assumption to challenge:** "the problem lives at the centre."
- **Evidence to seek:** what a farmer would need to believe to *not* travel.
- **Candidate type:** decision support over net realisation, with explicit uncertainty.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** if two candidates differ only in what is displayed, they are one.
- **Stopping condition:** stop when candidates start differing only by UI surface.

### E2 — Constraint exploitation: backpressure and credible commitment (PS-015)
- **Target region:** G2 — admission control with no service model.
- **Avoid:** building another token app; any design requiring new state-level IT integration.
- **Assumption to challenge:** "scheduling is a demand-side problem."
- **Constraint to exploit:** the centre already knows its bag/truck/space position each morning.
- **Candidate type:** mechanisms that make a slot a *backed* promise, including non-software ones.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** at least three candidates must work if the state agency never
  integrates anything.
- **Stopping condition:** when remaining ideas all require institutional adoption as step one.

### E3 — Incentive redesign: counter-interested attestation (PS-032)
- **Target region:** G4 — issuer and beneficiary of EPR evidence are the same party.
- **Avoid:** blockchain, QR-batch traceability, any ledger-first framing.
- **Assumption to challenge:** "better records fix fraud."
- **Evidence to seek:** who is *harmed* by a fake certificate and would therefore pay to detect it.
- **Candidate type:** mechanisms where a party with opposed interest is structurally required to
  co-sign, or where fraud becomes detectable statistically rather than documentarily.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** no candidate may rely on the honesty of the recycler.
- **Stopping condition:** when candidates reduce to "audit harder."

### E4 — Neglected stakeholder, inverted beneficiary (PS-032)
- **Target region:** G5 — the collector's interest as distinct from the collector's data.
- **Avoid:** smartphone apps for collectors; registration/formalisation schemes; directories.
- **Assumption to challenge:** "formalisation helps the collector."
- **Hard constraint:** ~70% of collectors have no smartphone; benefit must be immediate and in cash.
- **Candidate type:** designs where the collector's position improves per transaction and where
  participation is voluntary and reversible.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** if a candidate's benefit to the collector is "visibility," discard it.
- **Stopping condition:** when candidates stop being able to answer "why would he bother?"

### E5 — Human–machine boundary: severing the 7% gate (PS-023)
- **Target region:** G6 — every mechanism sits downstream of victim-initiated reporting.
- **Avoid:** panic buttons, SOS wearables, crime-data routing, gesture-triggered alerts.
- **Assumption to challenge:** "the victim will initiate."
- **Hard ethical constraint:** must not become passenger surveillance; must not create false
  accusation risk; must not expose the reporter. A candidate that ignores these is not a candidate.
- **Candidate type:** deferred disclosure, corroboration gating, bystander-side mechanisms,
  operator-side accountability, or changing what the *institution* must do rather than the victim.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** no candidate may require the victim to act during the incident.
- **Stopping condition:** when candidates can no longer clear the ethical constraint.

### E6 — Failure-mode-first, no new sensor (PS-028)
- **Target region:** G7 — verifying adequate pressure from vision.
- **Avoid:** adding a force sensor, instrumented glove, or smart tool (E7 may explore that instead).
- **Assumption to challenge:** "force must be measured to be verified."
- **Constraint to exploit:** correct pressure *deforms* the glass/sealant and *takes time*; effects
  of force may be observable even though force is not.
- **Candidate type:** observable-proxy designs, explicitly labelled as proxies, with a stated
  failure mode when the proxy decouples from the true quantity.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** each candidate must name the physical observable it depends on.
- **Stopping condition:** when the remaining proxies are not physically plausible.

### E7 — Cross-domain mechanism transfer (any Tier A problem)
- **Target region:** mechanisms listed as "present in adjacent fields but absent here" — escrow and
  bonded commitment, counter-interested attestation, queueing backpressure, deferred pooled
  disclosure, incoming-inspection pre-commitment testing — plus any others the explorer finds.
- **Avoid:** the source domain's vocabulary; transfer the mechanism, not the jargon.
- **Assumption to challenge:** that each of these problems needs a domain-specific invention.
- **Candidate type:** one mechanism, moved deliberately, with the transfer's breaking point named.
- **Anti-convergence rule:** each candidate must state what makes the target domain *different*
  from the source, and why the mechanism still works despite it.
- **Stopping condition:** when transfers become metaphorical rather than mechanical.
